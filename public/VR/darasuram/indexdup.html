UNESCO World Heritage — Chola Temples

Airavatesvara Temple, Darasuram

A Chola masterpiece where the chariot is stone, the horses are carved, and the stairs produce musical notes — Kumbakonam's greatest architectural wonder!

← Back to All Places
Main Gopuram
The entrance gopuram of Airavatesvara Temple is a smaller yet incredibly ornate structure. Built in the 12th century by Raja Raja Chola II, every inch is carved with exquisite detail — gods, flowers, and mythical beings.
The Stone Chariot (Mandapam)
The Mandapam is designed as a massive stone chariot pulled by horses and elephants! This is Darasuram's most iconic feature. The chariot symbolizes the Sun God's vehicle carrying Lord Shiva across the universe.
Musical Steps (Saptasvara Steps)
The famous musical steps at Darasuram are carved from a single stone. When tapped, the steps produce the seven musical notes — Sa, Re, Ga, Ma, Pa, Da, Ni! A stunning example of ancient acoustic engineering.
Main Mandapam Hall
The intricately carved mandapam showcases outstanding Chola craftsmanship. Notice the miniature sculptures of Yama (God of Death) riding a bull, and intricate scenes from the Ramayana and Mahabharata.
Main Sanctum — Airavatesvara
The main deity is Lord Shiva in the form of Airavatesvara — named because the divine white elephant Airavata (vehicle of Indra) worshipped Shiva here! The lingam inside is said to radiate cool, soothing energy.
